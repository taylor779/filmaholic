Filmaholic

Privacy policy

Effective 7 November 2026 · Below Studios

Filmaholic has no advertising and no tracking. The camera, the darkroom and your photos stay on your phone. What leaves it is what you choose to put in the community, which is public by design, and counts of what you use in the app, which you can turn off. This page explains each.

On your phone

Camera
To show your film look live in the viewfinder and to take photos and videos, including from the lock screen. Frames are processed on your iPhone and never sent anywhere.
Microphone
Only in video mode, to record sound with your videos.
Photos
To save what you take and export. If you allow it, Filmaholic also reads your library to keep its own photos together in a Filmaholic album and in Your photos, to show your library when you pick a photo to edit, and to open photos you pick. On Android, Filmaholic shows the photos it saved without any permission, and asks for access to your photos and videos only to show the rest of your library (All photos, Albums). This happens on your phone; your library isn't uploaded. Only a photo you choose to post leaves it (see below).
Photos shared to Filmaholic
When you share photos to Filmaholic from another app, copies are kept in the app's own storage until you've opened them, and are removed after a week if you don't.
Settings and looks
Your settings, saved looks, recordings waiting to finish, which film each of your photos was shot on, and a short technical log stay in the app's storage on your iPhone. Deleting the app removes them.

The community (optional)

The community lets you post photos, share film looks, enter photo challenges, comment on photos, follow people, and like and rate what other people make. You don't have to use it, and browsing doesn't need you to sign in. If you post, publish, comment, like, rate, follow, enter a challenge or set up a profile, Filmaholic stores that with Supabase, the database and file service we use for the community, on its servers in Sydney, Australia. iPhone and Android share the same community.

The first time you use the community, Filmaholic makes an account for it on your phone, without asking you to sign in: an identifier and a recovery key, and nothing else (no name, email or password). On iPhone they're kept in your iCloud Keychain, and on Android in your phone's Google backup (Block Store), so a new or reset phone is still you. We keep only a fingerprint of the key, not the key itself. If you used the community before November 2026, when it was stored in Apple's CloudKit, Filmaholic links that account to the identifier Apple gave it for your iCloud account, once, to bring what you'd posted across. That identifier is not your Apple ID or email address, and we can't see either.

What's stored, and who can see it:

  • Your username, shown on everything you post or publish. Public.
  • Your profile, if you set one up: a profile photo, your Instagram username and a short bio. Public, unless you make your profile private, which removes them from the community and leaves only your username.
  • Photos you post, with your caption, the film or look they were shot on and, if you enter one, the photo challenge. Only photos made in Filmaholic can be posted. Public.
  • Comments you write on photos, with your username and when you wrote them. Public. You can delete your own at any time, and the photo's maker can remove any comment on their photo.
  • Looks you publish: the name, the look's settings, the sample photo you choose, preview images, tags and any credit to the look it was based on. Public, to anyone using Filmaholic or opening a link to it.
  • Likes: each like is stored with your community account so it counts once; the number of likes on a photo is public.
  • Ratings and adds: the makers of looks you rate or add can be told, with your username.
  • Follows: who you follow. You can see who you follow, and the person you follow can see you in their list of followers if you have a username. How many followers someone has is public; who they are isn't.
  • Photos taken on community looks: when you take or export a photo on a look you added from the community, Filmaholic counts it for that look, with your community account, so its maker can see how much it's used. The first time, it also counts the photos already in Your photos that were shot on it. It's numbers only, never the photos. Only the totals are public: how many photos were taken on a look and by how many people. It goes with your usage counts: turning off Share usage counts stops it.
  • A device identifier: a random identifier Filmaholic makes on your phone and keeps with your community account, so that if an account is stopped from using the community, that stays true on the phone it was used on. It isn't used for anything else and isn't shared.
  • Reports you send: what you reported and why. Only our moderators see these.
  • Notifications (iPhone): if you turn them on, which ones you want (people adding or rating your looks, comments on your photos, new followers, new looks from people you follow, photos posted on your looks, a new photo challenge) and your phone's notification token, which Apple's push service uses to deliver them.

Photos you post or publish are re-encoded first, which removes their location and other metadata. Before a photo, profile photo or sample photo goes up, Filmaholic can check it for nudity on your iPhone, using Apple's Sensitive Content Analysis, if you've turned that on in Settings. The photo isn't sent anywhere to be checked. Android has no such check; reports are what keep the community clean there. Blocking or hiding someone is kept on your phone; blocking also lets our moderators know.

For a month after the move, what people post from older versions of Filmaholic (which still use CloudKit) is copied into the new community, so nothing they share is lost.

Usage counts

So we know what to improve, Filmaholic sends a count of what you did in the app each month: for example how many photos and videos you took, which of Filmaholic's films they were shot on, and how often you exported, imported or used the community. It's numbers only, never your photos, what you wrote or where you were. It's stored with Supabase, with the same community account, and only our moderators can see it (apart from the totals of photos taken on community looks, above). It isn't used for advertising or to track you across other apps. To stop it, go to the You tab, then Settings, and turn off Share usage counts.

Shared links and the App Clip

A shared look's link opens in Filmaholic, or in its App Clip if you don't have the app. The App Clip reads the public look without signing in. If you try it on a photo, the photo stays on your iPhone. The link's web page is hosted by GitHub Pages, which keeps its own server logs.

Moderation

Our moderators read reports, and can remove anything that breaks the community guidelines and stop an account from using the community. Something reported by several people stops being shown while we look at it.

Deleting your community account

In Filmaholic, go to the You tab, then Settings, then Delete my community account. This deletes your username and profile, your posts and challenge entries, your comments, the looks you've published, your likes, ratings and adds, who you follow and who follows you, the reports you sent, your notifications, your usage counts and your counts of photos taken on community looks, from the community and from what's left of it in Apple's CloudKit. Your looks and photos on your phone stay. If the account had been stopped from using the community, that stop and its device identifier are kept, so it still holds. You can also remove a single post or look at any time. If you can't use the app, email us and we'll delete it for you.

We keep community content until you delete it or we remove it under the guidelines.

Sharing and selling

We don't sell your data or share it with anyone for advertising. Community content is public because you publish it. Supabase stores it for us, on servers in Australia; see Supabase's privacy policy. Apple delivers notifications on iPhone, and stored the community before November 2026; see Apple's privacy policy. Under New Zealand's Privacy Act we only send personal information to a provider overseas that protects it in ways comparable to it.

Children

Apart from the usage counts above, the camera and darkroom collect nothing from anyone. The community isn't intended for children under 13.

Your rights

You can see, correct or delete what you've put in the community in the app, or ask us to. We handle personal information under New Zealand's Privacy Act 2020, and we'll help with requests under other privacy laws that apply to you.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update this page and the date at the top.
